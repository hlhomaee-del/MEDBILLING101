PART 2 · LAWS & REGULATIONS

Know the Law. Spot the Violation.

Click any law to expand its definition, penalties, and real examples. Then test yourself below.

HIPAA
Health Insurance Portability & Accountability Act
Tap to expand ↓
Protects: Patient health information (PHI) privacy and security.

Violation example: A biller texts patient diagnosis info to a friend.

Penalty: Up to $1.9M per violation category per year. Criminal charges possible.
FCA
False Claims Act
Tap to expand ↓
Protects: Government programs (Medicare/Medicaid) from fraudulent billing.

Violation example: A clinic bills Medicare for services never provided.

Penalty: Up to 3x the damages plus $22,000+ per false claim. Civil and criminal liability.
Stark Law
Physician Self-Referral Law
Tap to expand ↓
Protects: Prevents conflicts of interest in patient referrals.

Violation example: A doctor refers patients to a lab they own a financial interest in.

Penalty: Denial of claims, repayment, civil fines up to $15,000 per service.
HITECH
Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act
Tap to expand ↓
Protects: Strengthens HIPAA for electronic health records (EHR).

Violation example: A clinic's unencrypted laptop with patient records is stolen — and they fail to report the breach.

Penalty: Mandatory breach notifications. Increases HIPAA fines significantly.
FDCPA
Fair Debt Collection Practices Act
Tap to expand ↓
Protects: Patients from abusive debt collection practices.

Violation example: Collections agent calls a patient at 11pm repeatedly.

Penalty: Patient can sue for up to $1,000 plus attorney fees.
AKS
Anti-Kickback Statute
Tap to expand ↓
Protects: Prohibits paying for patient referrals for government-funded services.

Violation example: A pharmaceutical company pays a doctor to prescribe their drug.

Penalty: Felony criminal charges, up to 10 years per violation, $100K+ fines.

PART 4 · FRAUD vs ABUSE

Fraud or Abuse?

The key difference is intent. Drag each scenario to the correct category, or click to reveal.

Fraud
A deliberate deception or misrepresentation to obtain payment. INTENT is required. Always illegal.
Abuse
Practices that result in unnecessary costs without necessarily involving intentional deception. Often caused by poor training or sloppy processes.
⚠️
Important: Abuse can become fraud if the provider is warned and continues the behavior anyway. "I didn't know" stops working as a defense once you've been educated. The OIG takes repeated abuse very seriously.
PART 5 · COMPLIANCE

Building a Compliance Plan

Click each element of a compliance plan to see what it involves and why it matters.

🚨
OIG + Self-Disclosure Protocol (SDP)
OIG
Office of Inspector General. Investigates healthcare fraud, abuse, and compliance violations. Can exclude providers from Medicare/Medicaid permanently.
SDP — Self-Disclosure
If a clinic discovers it has overbilled or violated a rule — they should self-report through the OIG's Self-Disclosure Protocol BEFORE the government finds it. Penalties are typically lower.
Scenario: Your clinic discovers it accidentally billed Medicare for 50 services that were never fully documented. What do you do?
A. Hope no one notices and keep billing normally.
B. Quietly refund the amounts without reporting.
C. Use the OIG Self-Disclosure Protocol to self-report and repay.
D. Wait for the audit — then explain it was accidental.
PART 6 · CONSENT & AUDITS

Consent Types & Audit Methods

Understanding how patients authorize care — and how agencies verify it later.

Types of Patient Consent

TypeWhat It MeansExampleStatus
Informed Consent Patient fully understands the procedure, risks, benefits, and alternatives before agreeing. Surgeon explains surgery risks; patient asks questions and agrees. Required for procedures
Written Consent Patient signs a physical or electronic document authorizing treatment. Signing a treatment authorization form at check-in. Signed document
Implied Consent Patient's actions indicate agreement — no verbal or written consent given. Patient rolls up sleeve for a blood draw. Action-based

Audit Types — Who Watches the Watchers?

Internal Audit
Self-monitoring
The facility audits its own records, claims, and processes to catch errors before regulators do. Part of every strong compliance plan.

PART 7 · SYNTHESIS

The Full Case: Everything Goes Wrong

A provider has made multiple serious errors. Read the facts, then analyze the case.

Case Analysis Results

Predicted Consequences

Financial
Claim repayments + 3x damages under FCA. Potential exclusion from Medicare/Medicaid.
Legal
Criminal charges for HIPAA violations & FCA fraud. Civil penalties for Stark Law violations.
Operational
Mandatory compliance monitoring. Possible corporate integrity agreement with OIG.

Recommended Corrective Actions

1 File Self-Disclosure Protocol with OIG immediately for overbilled claims.
2 Conduct full HIPAA breach assessment and notify affected patients within 60 days.
3 Dissolve any financial interest in referred facilities to comply with Stark Law.
4 Implement mandatory coding and compliance training for all billing staff.
5 Establish internal audit schedule — quarterly billing reviews.
